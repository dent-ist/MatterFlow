# Dashboard > Users tab (issue #73, epic #68)

The fifth Dashboard tab shows the team's **workload** as one small tile per person (the firm has 100+ technicians), and (for people with `Dashboard.ViewCoverage`) a **Coverage** view: how many tasks each person completed per task type, for training and cross-coverage planning. Specification: `docs/ux/ux-spec.md`. Backend: `GET /api/v1/dashboard/workload` and `GET /api/v1/dashboard/coverage` (`docs/architecture.md` 8.5). Decision for the completion counts: `docs/requirements/decisions-addendum.md` section Q.

Screenshots were taken by `frontend/e2e/tests/users-screens.spec.ts` against the real stack (SQL Server in Docker, the published API with Dev auth serving the production Angular build, Chromium): **120 people** with the mix of a real firm (about a fifth idle, most with a light or normal load, twelve heavy, twelve out of office, six off hours, two pods), unassigned tasks, and a small coverage history. The accounts of the throwaway database have machine names, so the screenshot run swaps the display names in the workload response for ordinary sample names (nothing else is changed). Reproduce with:

```bash
E2E_SCREENSHOTS=docs/ux/dashboard-users frontend/e2e/run-local.sh -g "users screenshots"
```

## The tile

```
+--+-----------------------------+
|# | Name Surname           [+]  |   row 1: the name, truncated; [+] = Assign icon on hover or focus
|# | (|||| 7)          (clock 2)(!)|   row 2: pill = priority glyph + open count; marks: overdue, heavy, off hours
|# | [=====|====|===    ]        |   row 3: the load bar, split by priority (full = a Normal load or more)
+--+-----------------------------+
 ^ left edge in the priority colour
```

No sentences on a tile. The state is colour, shape and an icon; the words are in the accessible name and the tooltip (`title`) of the name button: *"Kofi Patel. Off hours. 10 open tasks, highest priority Critical, 2 overdue, 1 due soon, heavy load"*, *"Ana Diaz. Out of office until Nov 3. Annual leave. 2 open tasks, highest priority High, light load"*, *"Ben Khan. No open tasks"*.

| State | Look | Not colour alone |
|---|---|---|
| Highest open priority | Pill and left edge in the colours of the priority badge (token `critical`, `high`, `normal`, `low` or a `#RRGGBB` override, so renamed and recoloured priorities follow) | A glyph of four bars filled by rank (4 = most urgent, 1 = least), the same shape as the priority badge, and the count |
| Idle (no open tasks) | A very light tint of the Idle token (the token at 45% over the surface), a thin green outline, a check icon, an empty dashed bar (#133: a fact, not a trophy; saturated fills are for Heavy and Out of office) | Icon, dashed outline, no count |
| Out of office | Hatched grey tile, dashed outline, a calendar icon (and the open count when there is work) | Hatching, dashed outline, icon |
| Off hours | A moon mark | Icon |
| Overdue work | A red clock with the number | Icon and number |
| Heavy load | A red outline on the tile and a triangle mark; the bar is full | Outline, icon, bar |

Only the theme tokens and the priority badge colours are used (no new colours), and axe's colour contrast check passes on the tiles, the chips and the legend. The app has no dark theme yet; a theme would reach the tiles through the same tokens.

## Filters and legend

Quick filter chips under the toolbar (toggles with `aria-pressed`, a selected chip is also underlined). They are the first part of the legend, so the colours are learned where they are used:

- **Idle**, **Light**, **Normal**, **Heavy**: the load levels (thresholds `Dashboard:Workload:LightMax` 3 and `NormalMax` 8, in each chip's tooltip, its accessible name ("Light, 36. Light: 1 to 3 open tasks. 36 people.") and in the legend). They combine (any of the chosen levels) and are applied on the page (`uload=idle,heavy`). Idle is everyone who can take work with nothing open, including someone out of office.
- **Out of office** and **Off hours**: the availability filter (`uavail=out`, `uavail=off`; off hours is applied on the page, the API is asked for all).
- **Unassigned** moved into the capacity strip (below).
- Each chip shows how many people it holds in the current pod and name filter.

Toolbar (U2, #128), left to right, which is also the keyboard order: the **Workload | Coverage** switch (two text toggle buttons in a group with `aria-pressed`, not a second tablist because the tab row above is the page's tablist; only with `Dashboard.ViewCoverage`, otherwise none and the heading is "Workload"; `view=coverage`, default not written), search by name, **Highest priority** (`uprio`), **Availability** (All, Available, Off hours, Out of office), **Sort** (Availability and load, Name, Highest priority, Open tasks, Fewest open tasks), then clear filters (with the number of filters), **Tile size** (standard `pi-stop` or dense `pi-th-large`), **Layout** (cards `pi-id-card` or table `pi-table`) and **Legend** (the word is shown from 1100 px, the icon alone below). The icon buttons are 32 px toggles with `aria-pressed`, an `aria-label` that names the control and its state ("Tile size: dense", "Layout: table") and a text tooltip on hover and on keyboard focus (a CSS tip from `data-tip`, because `title` shows on hover only). One line at 1366, wraps without sideways scroll at 375; on Coverage the first control is the same switch.

**Capacity strip (U1, #127)** above the chips: one bar stacked by load level (Idle, Light, Normal, Heavy; each segment's width is its share of the people on the board, its icon always and its number when it fits). Each segment is a toggle button named "Filter: 21 heavy" that sets the same filter as the chip of that level (`aria-pressed`, a second click clears); the chips stay and show the same counts. The strip is a group whose name is the whole text summary ("Capacity: 24 idle, 36 light, 48 normal, 12 heavy, 12 out of office"). Out of office is counted by its chip (it overlaps the levels). At its right: "N unassigned tasks" (a link to the Task Queue unassigned filter, with the scope's `pod`; the old Unassigned chip moved here) and, with `Task.Assign`, the **Assign work** menu with two entries: "Show people who can take work now" (Availability = Available, load Idle and Light, sorted "Fewest open tasks") and "Open the unassigned queue". No automatic assignment and no ranking. The default sort stays "Availability and load"; the strip makes the heavy end visible. Heavy keeps its outline and icon; the Idle segment uses the Idle tint.

**Who is counted (U4, #130).** The Workload view lists people who can take work: active users who are members of at least one active pod or who hold at least one open task (a person holding work is never hidden, so an overloaded person outside every pod stays visible; "has Task.Assign" is not the rule, it is the permission to assign others). The rule is applied on the server (`includeNoPod`, sent as `false` by default, `true` with `unopod=1`; the response carries `hiddenCount`), so chips, the strip, the tile totals, the Out of office count and the unassigned figure follow it. A quiet line under the strip says "Showing 478 people who can take work. 42 others are hidden. Show them" (a link button; with the switch on it says "Including people without a pod. Hide them"). Clear filters leaves the switch alone; under a Pod scope the list is that pod's members, nobody is hidden and the line is not shown. The Assign picker and Coverage are not affected (a person without a pod can be assigned work and appears in Coverage if they completed tasks). The legend has a "Who is counted" paragraph.

The legend (the info icon, "Legend" at 1100 px and wider) opens a panel with the pill colours and their level glyphs, the idle and out of office looks, the marks, the load thresholds, a hint to hover or focus a tile for its whole status, and what the view is not.

## Decisions

- **Colour follows the highest priority, with a shape.** The same colour pair as the priority badge, plus the four-bar glyph by rank, so Critical, High, Normal and Low are told apart without colour. Idle is green, out of office is hatched grey; off hours is a small mark and does not change the tile.
- **Segmented load bar** split by priority (legend order) along the bottom of the tile. The track is as wide as the Normal ceiling, or as the open total when there are more tasks than that.
- **Operational framing.** The Workload view has no completion counts, speed, ranking or performance measures, and says so in the legend (the always-visible sentence was removed to give the room to the tiles). The Coverage view is the one exception, decided by the product owner (addendum Q) and gated by its own permission.
- **Filters:** availability, name and the population switch (`includeNoPod`) are sent to the API; load, highest priority and off hours are applied on the page (no new read). All are in the address and cleared when another tab is chosen. Sort, tile size and the view are remembered per user (guarded `localStorage`).
- **Keyboard model.** Deliberately simple, as before: the tiles are an ordinary list in reading order with the normal tab order. A tile has two tab stops, the person's name (a button that opens the drawer; its click area is the whole tile, with a visible focus ring) and the Assign icon. The Assign icon is revealed on hover and on keyboard focus of the tile (it is always reachable by Tab); on a touch screen it is hidden and the drawer has it. The name is not a heading, so a screen reader does not meet 120 headings. The accessible name of the button starts with the visible name and then gives the state in words.
- **Drawer.** A modal right-hand drawer like the Swimlane one: focus moves to Close, is trapped, Esc returns it to the tile. It lists the person's open tasks from the Task Queue, sorted overdue first, then priority, then due date (the rule is stated under the heading), 100 at a time. A row is two lines: number, task type icon and title; then status, priority and the due chip (`mf-due-chip`, "Overdue 3d" in text). Status and priority keep their inline menus; the assignee menu is replaced by one icon button "Reassign TSK-123" (shown only with `Task.Assign`) that opens the same people picker. After a reassignment the row leaves, the counts update, focus moves to the next row's Reassign button and a live region says "TSK-123 reassigned to Ben Grant". "Open in Task Queue" and "Assign work" are icon buttons with names and tooltips.
- **Assign work.** From the tile (icon) and the drawer: a modal picker of unassigned open tasks, highest priority first then oldest (stated under the heading). By default it lists the unassigned tasks of the person's pods; the "All pods" switch (remembered for the session) shows everything, and a person in no pod gets All pods with the note "<name> is not in a pod." The page Scope is not applied. A row shows the task type icon, number, title, priority, client, matter, due chip and age. "Assign the next 3" takes the first three rows and asks "Assign these 3 tasks to <name>?" with the task list and the out-of-office or Heavy warning repeated; checkboxes pick other tasks ("Assign N selected", at most 10). The confirmed tasks go in one request, `POST /api/v1/tasks/assign-batch` (permission `Task.Assign`, at most 10, one ordinary assignment per task with its own audit entries and New to In Progress, a result per task; a task that changed meanwhile is reported as not assigned and the others are kept). Success announces "3 tasks assigned to <name>." and offers Undo; the tile counts update at once. A visible hint appears when the person is out of office or Heavy; assigning is still allowed.
- **Many people.** 200 tiles are drawn at first (Show more adds 200, Show all draws the rest; icon buttons with the count in the sentence next to them). A tile is about twenty small elements and no per-tile subscriptions, so 450 people were drawn in the unit test without trouble.
- **Refresh.** The refresh icon sits in the Dashboard's tab row (as on the Swimlane tab) and reads the tiles or the coverage matrix again. The Workload view also refreshes itself every 5 minutes while the tab is visible, skipped while a menu, the drawer or a dialog is open, and it never moves focus. A failed background read keeps the tiles and shows a banner with a Retry icon.
- **Phone.** At 375 px the tiles are two to a line, the chips are one line that scrolls sideways inside itself, and the toolbar wraps.

### Coverage view

- **Where:** a "Workload | Coverage" switch at the top of the tab (only with `Dashboard.ViewCoverage`), `?tab=users&view=coverage`, remembered. Reaching it needs the Users tab permission (`Dashboard.ViewUsers`) as well; the server needs `Dashboard.View` + `Dashboard.ViewCoverage`.
- **Heading:** "Task type coverage - use this to plan training and cross-coverage, not to rank people". No score, no "best / worst" wording.
- **Orientation (WP11, C1 #134):** a text switch "By task type | Matrix" first in the Coverage toolbar (`aria-pressed` buttons). **By task type is the default**; the matrix writes `cov=matrix` to the address (the default is not written). By task type is a real table (cards below 561 px): a disclosure button, Task type (icon, name, Inactive tag), Handled by (the first five names, alphabetical, plain text, then "+N"; "No one in this period"), People, Completed and Status ("No one" neutral with an icon, "One person" amber with an icon, blank otherwise). Order: "Fewest people first" (default, ties by name), "Type name", "Most completed" (remembered per user). The search box finds a task type or a person by name. Expanding a row (`aria-expanded`) lists everyone with the number of tasks, each number opens the tasks behind it in the Coverage drawer ("Open in Task Queue"), and "Show people in <pod> who have not handled this type" lists the scope pod's members with a neutral 0 (with all pods it says "Pick a pod in Scope ..."). At most 60 types are drawn, "Show N more task types" adds more. At 375 px each type is a card (name, status, "N people, M completed") with the names behind "Show people". The data come from `GET /dashboard/coverage?groupBy=type` (no people columns or cells).
- **Two statements (C3 #136)** above both orientations: a neutral collapsed "N task types had no completed tasks in this period." (hint: "Rarely used types can be empty in a short period. Try a longer period.", with the type names) and, only above zero, an amber `role="status"` line "N task types were handled by only one person in this period: <types>. Consider cross-training." Zero is informational, exactly one is the warning (addendum Q amended). The words "gap" and "bus factor" are not used there.
- **The matrix (C2 #135):** a real table. Rows are task types (icon and name), columns are people (initials and name; Inactive and Out of office are small markers), the last column is Total and "People who handled it" ("No one" / "One person" statuses), the last row is the column totals. The first column and the header row are sticky inside the matrix's own scrolling region. At first 30 people and 60 task types are drawn ("Show 30 more people", "Show 60 more task types"); the totals always cover everyone.
- **Cells:** the number is written in every non-zero cell; **a zero is blank** on a light neutral background (no hatch, no dashed edge) and has the accessible name "Ana B., Redaction: 0 completed ..." and a tooltip; it is focusable but opens nothing. Possible training gaps (a zero for someone who completed other work) are outlined in amber with an icon **only while the "Training gaps" toggle is on** (default off; it highlights, it no longer filters), and the strip then says "N possible training gaps". The legend says "Blank = 0 in this period" and "Outline = possible training gap". Five steps (1-2, 3-5, 6-10, 11+) in one blue family; the numbers reach 4.5:1. The scale is **relative to the task type** by default or absolute. Every non-zero cell has the name "Priya: 7 Relativity Production tasks completed in the last 90 days".
- **Counting:** completed tasks (not cancelled) whose completion time is inside the period in the viewer's time zone, for the person the task is assigned to (the task keeps no separate "completed by"). Period: 30 days (default), 90 days, 6 months, 12 months or a custom range. The page-wide Scope (members of the pod; there is no Pod control in the view any more, P1) and the name filter; row and column order by name or total (matrix). A cell or a count opens the tasks behind it with "Open in Task Queue".
- **Keyboard:** the matrix is one tab stop; arrow keys, Home and End move between cells, Enter or Space opens a cell.

## UX review (self-critique, and what changed)

Checked at 120 people against the screenshots:

- **Scan-ability.** The first version put the overdue and heavy marks beside the pill, which squeezed the load bar to 25 px on a heavy person; the bar now runs along the bottom of the tile and the marks sit opposite the pill. The default sort lists idle people first (they are who a coordinator assigns to), so the first screen is mostly green; the Heavy chip answers "who is overloaded" in one click. The page header, toolbar and chips take about 250 px, so 120 standard tiles are about 1.3 screens (one screen when dense).
- **Colour-blind safety.** Normal and Low are both neutral greys in the badge palette; the level glyph (2 bars against 1, now 3 px wide) and the count tell them apart, and every state has an icon (check, calendar, clock, triangle, moon) or a pattern (hatching, dashed outline). A selected chip is underlined as well as outlined (the green Idle chip changed colour too little to show it otherwise).
- **Tooltip discoverability.** The tooltip is the browser's `title` on the name button (also the accessible name), which appears after a short delay on hover only. To make up for that the legend says to hover or focus a tile, the Assign icon appearing on hover marks the tile as live, a click opens the drawer with everything, and a keyboard or screen reader user gets the full sentence as the button name. Known limit: a sighted keyboard user does not see the `title`; they see the drawer on Enter.
- **Fixed during the review:** the legend button had both `aria-pressed` and `aria-expanded` (now only `aria-expanded`); the Assign icon hidden by opacity is checked by axe in the suite; the chips of a name-filtered board counted the whole team for Out of office (now counted the same way as the others); the name was a heading on every tile (now plain text); the Sort select clipped its text.
- **Not done:** a sticky toolbar while scrolling 120 tiles (it would cost 100 px of the screen), and a virtualised list (200 tiles at a time were enough).

## Checks

Vitest specs (colour and priority mapping including renamed priorities, tile content and the absence of sentences, marks, the chips and their filters, the address, sorting, drawer, assign flow, 450 people drawn 200 at a time, coverage steps, scales, totals, gaps), Playwright against the real stack (tile states, colours and glyphs by priority, the accessible name and tooltip, the size of a tile, chips and filters with the address, table, drawer focus, inline edit and Undo, assign flow with hints and Undo, hover and keyboard reveal of the Assign icon, legend, responsive, the Coverage matrix, its drawer and permission), axe on every view (cards, legend, table, drawers, the matrix), and the bundle budget (the tab and its dialogs are one lazy chunk; the initial bundle did not grow).

**Scope (P1, issue #89).** The Pod filter of the Users tab (`upod`) was replaced by the page-wide Scope select in the tab row (`?pod=`, see ux-spec S01): Workload lists the members of the scope's pods and its Unassigned chip counts (and links to the Task Queue with the same `pod` filter) the unassigned open tasks of the scope; a person's own open counts still cover all of their open tasks. Coverage lists the members of the scope's pods. An old `upod=<id>` link is read once into `pod` and rewritten. The workload endpoint also takes `includeNoPod` and answers `hiddenCount` for the "can take work" rule (#130); omitted, everyone is listed (the page always sends it).

**WP10a (#130, #128, #127, #133).** Frontend only (the endpoint rule shipped with WP4, so no API, OpenAPI or SQL change). New components `users-views.ts` (the text switch) and `users-capacity.ts` (strip and menu); `users-toolbar.ts`, `users-summary.ts`, `users-card.ts` (Idle tint, also the Idle pill in the table and drawer through `bandOf`), `users-model.ts` (`includeNoPod`, `unopod`, `capacitySegments`, `loadHelp`, `fewest` sort) and `dash-users-tab.ts` changed. Idle tint: the existing token `--mf-success-bg` at 45% over the surface (`color-mix`, no new token); text `--mf-success` on it is above 4.5:1 and the outline above 3:1 (unit test with the token values, axe at 1366, 1920 and 375). Screenshots in `../dashboard-review-2/fixes/wp10a/`; the e2e is `frontend/e2e/tests/users-capacity.spec.ts` (population rule with pod-less people, equal counts in chips, strip and API, scope, the menu, keyboard order, one line at 1366, axe; screenshots with `WP10A_SHOTS=<folder>`). Known limit: at 375 px the app shell's rail leaves 271 px, so the strip is 271 px wide.
