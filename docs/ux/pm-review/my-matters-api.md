# My Matters API as built (WP-PM2, epic #667)

The contract is `contracts/openapi/v1.json`; this page records the decisions where the build refined the plan in `README.md` section 5 and 6. The routes are in `MyMattersController` (WorkViews) and `MatterBulkTasksController` (WorkManagement).

## Access
- `GET/POST /api/v1/my-matters/*` need **`Task.View` and (`Matter.BeManager` or `Matter.AssignManagers`)**. A manager without `Task.View` gets 403 (the pages show task data and every count opens the Task Queue, which needs `Task.View`).
- `scope=all` and `managerId` need `Matter.AssignManagers` (403 `PERMISSION_DENIED`, `meta.permission`). `managerId` without `scope=all` is a 400.
- "My matters" is a default view, not access control: nothing here hides a matter from anybody who can view tasks.

## Common parameters
`scope=mine|all`, `role=both|primary|secondary`, `clientId`, `from`, `to`, `managerId` (All scope only), `fresh=true` (skip the summary cache). Unknown parameters are a 400. The viewer's time zone (`X-Time-Zone`) decides overdue and "due in N days", as in My Work.

- **`from` / `to` are a due-date range on the tasks**: with a range only open tasks with an explicit due date inside it are counted or listed (ASAP tasks have none), and every Task Queue link carries `dueFrom` / `dueTo`, so a count still equals its list. Overdue tasks fall outside a range that starts today or later; the page should send the range to the deadlines and activity calls only if it wants overdue work in the strip. On the activity call the range replaces the 7-day window (at most 90 days back).
- **No "No project manager" filter** was added to the All scope: the Task Queue has no equivalent filter, so counts could not equal their lists, and adding one would change the generated positional client methods of the Task Queue. The Matters list has `hasNoManager` already.

## Definitions
- **Unassigned** = open and no person (a pod may be set): the Task Queue's `unassigned=true` and the Dashboard's definition. (The plan text "no person and no pod" would not equal its link.)
- **Matters** = active matters in the scope. Inactive matters cannot hold open tasks (deactivation is refused while any task is open).
- **Flag**: Red = any open overdue task; Amber = no overdue but an open task is unassigned, or due within 3 days, or on hold longer than 7 days; Green otherwise; a matter with no open tasks is Green with reason `NO_OPEN_TASKS`. `flagReasons` lists every branch that applies. Manager state never enters it (`MyMattersRules.Flag`).
- **% workflow complete** = steps COMPLETED or SKIPPED over all steps of the workflow instances of the matter's open tasks, whole percent rounded down; null with no open task.
- **Stuck on hold** = On Hold since more than 7 full days (fixed). The attention group's "View all" query lists *all* tasks on hold, oldest first (the Task Queue has no age filter), so its total can exceed the group total.
- **On hold in the strip** carries `oldestHoldDays` and `oldestHoldSinceUtc`.

## Activity and the unread marker
Kinds: `TASK_CREATED`, `STATUS_CHANGED` (from/to status codes), `REASSIGNED` (from/to names), `COMMENTED` (ordinary comments only, **never the text**), `MANAGER_CHANGED` (`managerPlace`, from/to names). The marker is the highest audit event id the person marked as read, in `dbo.UserMatterActivitySeen` (script 31); the Notifications store keeps one flag per notification and was not reusable. The first call of a person creates the marker at the newest event (nothing unread on the first visit); a person's own actions are never unread; the marker only moves forward.

## Bulk change: `POST /api/v1/matters/{matterId}/tasks/bulk`
Body `{taskIds[1..100], reassignTo?: {userId?, podId?}, priorityId?}`. A person alone keeps each task's pod; a pod alone moves the task to the pod's queue and clears its person; both set both. Needs `Task.Assign` for `reassignTo` and `Task.SetPriority` for `priorityId` (a missing one is a 403 for the whole request, nothing changes). Request-level problems (size, duplicates, nothing to change, unknown or inactive person, pod or priority, unknown matter) are 400/404 before anything is touched. Then each task is its own transaction through the single-task commands (assignment + priority atomic per task, auto-start on assignment, notifications and audit as for one task). The answer lists every task in request order: `{taskId, ok, code, message, warnings}` with codes `NOT_FOUND`, `NOT_IN_MATTER`, `TASK_NOT_EDITABLE` (completed or cancelled; New, In Progress and On Hold can change), `CONCURRENCY_CONFLICT`; the cross-pod case is the warning `USER_NOT_IN_POD`. `changed`, `failed` and one `correlationId` (shared by every audit entry of the batch) complete the answer. No undo.

## Epic #758 additions (backend, WP-PX2)

### Deadlines grouped (PX-07, #765)
`GET /my-matters/deadlines` gains `groupBy` (absent, `day` or `matter`; anything else is a 400 `UNKNOWN_GROUP_BY`). Without it the answer is the flat 25-row list of #681, unchanged. With it the flat `items` list is empty and one more field is filled (`groupBy` echoes the choice):
- `groupBy=day`: `byDay` has **one entry per calendar day from today to the last day of the window** (15 entries for `days=14`, 29 for `days=28`; days with no deadline have `count` 0), each with `date`, `isWeekend` (Saturday/Sunday are included, muted by the page), `isToday`, `count`, the first five tasks (`items`, soonest first) and the Task Queue `query` that lists exactly the counted tasks (`asap=false&overdue=false&dueFrom=D&dueTo=D`, plus the scope parts; the scope's own `from`/`to` are not repeated). The day is the task's calendar due date, which is the viewer's day. `total` is the sum of the counts.
- `groupBy=matter`: `byMatter` has the matters that have a deadline in the window, soonest first, at most 50 (`matterTotal` says how many matters have one), each with `total`, `nextDueDate`, its next five tasks and a `query` (`matter=<id>` on the window query). `total` is all tasks in the window.
- Statements: a grouped count, one `CROSS APPLY TOP 5` per distinct date (days) or per matter (matter grouping: a seek of `IX_Task_Matter` per matter), then the task columns and the names; none depends on the number of matters. Visibility: the scope is the same as everywhere in My Matters (the shared seam).

### Workload with away windows and the matter split (PX-08, #766)
`GET /my-matters/workload` keeps its shape and adds, per person row: `dueThisWeek` (open, not overdue, due today to +7 days: the strip's "due in 7 days"), `away` (the saved out-of-office period that overlaps today and the next 28 days: `from`, `to` as calendar days, `null` for an open start or end, and `reasonCode`, which is always `OUT_OF_OFFICE` because the availability profile records no other reason; **the free-text note is never returned here**) and, only with `include=matters`, `matters` (`top`: the five matters with most open tasks of that person, ties by matter reference, each with `open`, `overdue` and a Task Queue `query`; `otherMatters` and `otherOpen` for the rest). The existing `availability` field is unchanged and still means "out of office right now". `overdue` and `away` are two plain facts side by side: nothing flags or ranks the combination. People with no open task on these matters are not listed, away or not. `include` other than `matters` is a 400.

Statements: the grouped counts (now with the due-this-week count), the pod and people names, the availability evaluation, **one** read of the away windows for all the people (`IUserAvailabilityReader.AwayWindowsAsync`, new in Identity.Contracts: the profiles that overlap the range, without the note) and, for the split, one grouped statement by (pod, person, matter) plus one name read; none depends on the number of matters or people.

### Activity: 30 days, filters, cursor paging, and "Since your last visit" (PX-10, #768)
`GET /my-matters/activity` now looks back **30 days** by default (7 in epic #667; a `from`/`to` range still replaces the window, at most 90 days back) and adds `matterId` (one of the scope's matters; any other id gives an empty list), `kind` (repeatable or comma separated, ORed: `TASK_CREATED`, `STATUS_CHANGED`, `COMPLETED` and `ON_HOLD` for the status changes to that status, `REASSIGNED`, `COMMENTED`, `MANAGER_CHANGED`; an unknown kind is a 400), `unread=true` (only what `isUnread` marks) and `cursor` (the previous page's `nextCursor`; the answer has `nextCursor` when `hasMore`). The cursor is the event id, so a page continues exactly where the previous one stopped, newest first, whatever arrives meanwhile. `unreadCount` is still the count for the whole window, whatever the matter or kind filter says. Comment text is never read.

`GET /my-matters/since-last-visit` (common parameters only) answers "what changed since the person's marker": `counts` of `newTasks`, `completed`, `becameOverdue`, `reassigned`, `putOnHold` and `comments`, each as `{count, query, target}`, and up to five `items` (a task that became overdue first, then the newest holds and reassignments; `kind` `BECAME_OVERDUE`, `ON_HOLD` or `REASSIGNED`, with `task` or `event` filled). The period starts at the time of the person's last "Mark as read" (`hasMarker`), or 7 days ago without one (`hasMarker` false, `seenEventId` 0); never earlier than 30 days. The marker moves **only** on `POST /my-matters/activity/seen`: this call neither moves nor creates it. The person's own actions are not counted. The activity counts come from one grouped read of the audit events after the marker (a clustered range on the event id, scope matters, the visibility rule for history written while a matter was sensitive); `target` is `activity` and `query` is the query string of `GET /my-matters/activity` that lists the same events (`kind=...&unread=true`; without a marker, `kind=...&from=<day>` because the list has no marker to compare with). *Became overdue* is derived, not an event: open tasks of the scope that are overdue now and whose due moment (the due sort key: the instant, or the end of the due day) fell inside the period; `target` is `taskQueue` and the query is `overdue=true&dueFrom=<first day>` plus the scope, so on the first day of the period it can list a few tasks that were already overdue earlier that day.

### Trends (PX-09, #767)
`GET /my-matters/trends?weeks=8|12|26` (default 8; anything else is a 400 `UNKNOWN_WEEKS`; the common parameters apply, the `from`/`to` range is accepted and ignored because the weeks are the range). One point per week, **Monday to Sunday in the viewer's zone**, the current week last (`isCurrent`): `created` (tasks created in the week, by `CreatedAt`), `closed` (tasks completed in the week, by the moment of the status change, the Dashboard's definition), `overdueAtWeekEnd` (open at the end of the week, or now for the current week, with a due moment before it) and the Task Queue queries `createdQuery` / `closedQuery` that list exactly the counted tasks (the open-ended current week carries no `…To`). `now` has today's figures: `open`, `averageAgeDays` (now minus `CreatedAt` of the open tasks, one decimal), `onHold`, `oldestOnHoldDays` and their queries.

**Approximate by design (owner decision), and the answer says so**: `approximate` is always true and `limitations` lists why: `CURRENT_DUE_DATES` (a task that was re-dated is counted by its current due date in every week), `CURRENT_STATUS` (a task that was re-opened is counted by its current status and last status change), `CURRENT_MATTER_SET` (the matters are those in scope now, with all their tasks). The schema keeps no history of due dates or statuses and nothing was added to keep one; if the measurement of #770 shows the derivation is too slow, a nightly snapshot table is the documented fallback (`dbo.MatterDailyStats`). `created` and `closed` are exact for the tasks that still exist.

Statements: four, whatever the number of matters, tasks or weeks: one grouped read of the open tasks (count, average age, on hold, oldest hold), the creation instants in the range, the completion instants in the range, and the tasks with a due moment that were open at some point of the range (id-free columns only); the weeks are filled in memory (week edges are not UTC edges, so SQL cannot group by week). The week arithmetic is a pure class, `MyMattersTrendRules`, with unit tests.

### Matter overview (PX-11, #769)
`GET /matters/{matterId}/overview` (policy `Matter.View`) gives the matter record's Overview in one round trip: `matter` (reference, name, client, active, sensitive, Primary and Secondary manager or null: **no manager is normal and never a warning, there is no such field**), then sections that are **null when the caller lacks the permission** (never an empty list): `taskCounts` (New, In progress, On hold, Completed in the last 30 days, Open, each with the Task Queue query that lists the counted tasks), `deadlines` (the next five open, not overdue tasks with a due date, `total` and the query that lists all of them), `people` (the ten people with most open tasks on the matter, `total` people, `unassignedOpen`, each with open, overdue, the away window of the next 28 days as dates and a code, never the note, and a query), `activity` (the last ten events of the last 90 days; the unread marker is neither created nor moved), `health` (the My Matters flag and its reasons as codes with counts and queries: `OVERDUE`, `UNASSIGNED`, `DUE_SOON`, `LONG_HOLD`, or `NO_OPEN_TASKS`; it is `MyMattersRules.Flag`, so the matter list and this page agree) and, with `Asset.View`, `assets` (the number of active assets and the first five by name). The task sections need `Task.View`; without it only the header (and the assets) are returned.

Access: any caller who may view the matter, whether or not they manage it. A matter the caller may not see (the sensitive-matter rule) or that does not exist answers 404. The tasks are the visible tasks. Statements are constant (about fifteen): the visibility check, the header with client, the manager names, **one** grouped pass over the matter's tasks for the status counts and every health figure, the deadlines (count and top five), the people (one grouped read, the names, one batch of away windows), the assets (count and top five), the audit range (one seek of the time index, one page read, the names of the page).
